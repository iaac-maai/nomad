# Review of 29 Oct 2026: what the critics said, what it means, what we could change

For discussion first. Nothing below has been built. Transcripts: three recordings, transcribed with Whisper. Reviewer
names are not in the recordings, so they are called Critic A, B and C here.

---

## 1. What the critics said (in short)

**Critic A (recording 1): viability and narrative**
- "Who owns these shops?" There are many owners. Why would they turn a shop into (social) housing?
- Who adopts NOMAD? Probably **policy makers**: a city that must house X people to a minimum standard, fast.
- Don't present it naively ("shops to houses"). Find the logic and be ready to defend it.
- Look for **precedents**: has this been done elsewhere, in emergencies? Bring numbers and values that show it can work.
- Option to frame it as **temporary / emergency housing**: a few months, packable.
- Permits for a change of use are complicated, and most owners are not connected to the process.
- Presentation: open with a **short, sharp video of the specific problem** (not the general housing crisis).

**Critic B (recording 2): liveability, the documents, Grasshopper**
- "On track." Narrowing the scope was good.
- **Don't make homes that aren't liveable.** The plan with no windows "is like a hospital… people don't need to live in a jail."
  A room without a window is not legally a room. Height matters too. Ask: what is a liveable space?
- The constraints should reveal the **"blue spots"**: the parts of a shop that really are liveable (the sun analysis
  drawing where only one part is liveable is "the spot of the research").
- The sun analysis takes time, so **show it, with lots of images**.
- **Mixed use:** a street can't be only housing. A result can be "this place should not be converted": tell the decision
  maker to avoid it and move on.
- **The rulebook:** "put those documents on the screen". Show how hard they are to read, and that they are in Catalan,
  Spanish, Basque… AI makes them accessible. He wants that database (compares it with his NotebookLM).
- **Grasshopper:** a node with 15 to 50 inputs is not usable. Break it into small nodes, each doing one operation.
- Remove a lot of data from the slides. Show the problem: a good room and a bad room of Barcelona, side by side.

**Critic C (recording 3): the opportunity and the zoom-in**
- Contrast a bright, breathing space with a terrible one: that is reality.
- **Highlight the opportunity**: the empty premises on a full-screen map. That is the assumption the project rests on.
- Go wider: not just this part of Barcelona, but the metropolitan area and the rest of Spain.
- Then **zoom in**: not all are usable (too deep, etc.), down to the ones NOMAD can solve.
- Back to viability: "Do you want to live in a place where it hurts?"
- Again: temporary housing for families displaced by a disaster (e.g. flash floods) is one possible framing.

## 2. What it means for NOMAD

| Theme | Where we are | What is missing |
|---|---|---|
| Who uses it | Website for owners + a city-wide map | A clear **policy-maker** story: "the city needs N homes; NOMAD shows where, how many, and which shops to avoid" |
| Owners | Census = empty premises, no owner data | Owner type is not open data (Catastro titularity is protected). We can say who *decides* (owner + community of owners, LPH) and what the incentive is (value estimate already on the site) |
| Precedents | None in the deck | 2 to 4 real cases of shop-to-home or emergency conversions, with numbers (to research; nothing invented) |
| Liveability | Rules checked; daylight bands simple | Make "not liveable" a first-class result (the NO / "avoid" answer), and show the liveable "blue spots" from Bars's daylight model |
| Mixed use | Every empty shop is treated alone | A street-level check: share of ground floors already housing, so NOMAD can warn "this street would lose its shops" |
| Scale | Barcelona city, 6,149 premises | The AMB area and Spain as context (one slide), then zoom in: 6,149 → 5,026 zone OK → 1,694 clean shells → 1,192 with a home |
| The rulebook | Flipbook of 1,408 original pages, index, project bookmarks (built today) | Show it in the talk: the pages, the languages, then NOMAD's plain answer |
| Grasshopper | One big component with many inputs | Split into small components: read plan, zoning, rules, generate, 3D, report |
| Presentation | Data-heavy | Open with a short video of the specific problem; fewer numbers; a good room vs a bad room |

## 3. Proposed changes (to decide together)

1. **Narrative:** policy maker first. "Barcelona needs homes fast; 6,149 shops are empty; NOMAD says which can become decent
   homes, how many, and which to avoid." Owners second, with the value estimate as their incentive.
2. **Temporary housing as a second scenario** (Critic A and C): same checks, but for a limited period. We need to find what
   the rules say about temporary use before we promise anything (no rule invented).
3. **Precedents slide:** research real conversions (Spain and abroad) with numbers.
4. **"Avoid" as a result:** the NO answer becomes a map layer: "do not convert: not liveable / street would lose its shops".
5. **Liveable spots:** Bars's daylight model in the check (still waiting for access to his repository), shown as the "blue spots".
6. **Mixed-use check per street** (from the census: share of ground floors that are shops vs homes).
7. **Opening:** zoom from Spain → AMB → Barcelona → the 6,149 → the ones NOMAD can solve; a good room vs a bad room.
8. **Rulebook on screen** (done): show the documents in their languages, then the plain answer.
9. **Grasshopper:** split NOMAD.gh into small nodes (one operation each), so it is readable and reusable.
10. **Slides:** cut the data; one message per slide; a short problem video.

## 4. Finch3D (finch3d.com/product): what their interface does, and what fits NOMAD

**How their site is built:** a white page, a large serif headline ("Redefining architectural workflows"), one short
sentence, then four promises (encode design systems, generate with precision, explore more, deliver faster). Each section
is one claim plus a looping product video. There are logos of the tools it connects to (Rhino, Revit, Grasshopper, Forma,
Archicad), one customer quote and a "Book a demo" button. It uses little text and lets the product videos do the work.

**How their app is laid out** (from the Finch docs, "Finch Interface", "Iterate with variants"):
- **Centre:** the 3D model, with view controls at the top (wireframe etc.).
- **Left: Key Figures.** A ring chart of the programme (residential / retail / circulation) and totals (GFA, NIA,
  usable area); hover a segment for the number. The list of **variants** also lives here.
- **Right: Data drawers.** Programmes, space types, unit mix, story heights and walls, each one an expandable drawer.
- **Studios you double-click into:** Massing → Floor plate → Plan editor (draw walls, doors, objects).
- **Variants side by side,** compared on key figures, with a **parallel-axis chart** across variants.
- Sun with date and time, an AI agent (Archie) for repetitive fixes, PDF export.

**What could work for NOMAD's website**

| Finch idea | NOMAD version | Effort |
|---|---|---|
| Product-page style: one claim + one moving picture per section | Home page: 4 promises (find, check, design, decide) each with a short loop of NOMAD itself | Medium (needs screen recordings) |
| App layout: 3D centre, key figures left, drawers right | "Design homes" as one workspace: 3D/plan in the middle; left = verdict + key figures (homes, m², flats passing, value); right = drawers (your requirements, rule cards, laws for this project, report) | Medium–high (a re-layout of an existing section) |
| Ring chart of the programme | Ring: living / bedrooms / kitchen-bath / circulation / unused, per layout | Low |
| Variants list + parallel-axis comparison | Our 3 layouts + saved shops, compared on homes, passing flats, façade use, daylight | Medium |
| Double-click to go deeper (massing → floor → unit) | City → shop → layout → flat | Medium |
| Sun date/time slider | Already in the city 3D; add it to the layout 3D | Low |
| AI agent (Archie) | A "why?" assistant that explains a failed rule in plain words, citing the rulebook page | Medium (needs a model call) |
| Tool logos / interoperability | Rhino + Grasshopper, IFC, glTF, PDF | Low |

**What not to copy:** Finch is an enterprise authoring tool for architects (BIM export, templates, SSO). NOMAD's users are
a policy maker and an owner: plain language and the verdict must stay first, and the law visible.
