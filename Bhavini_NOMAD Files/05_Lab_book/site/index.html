E01
Stage1 Rulebook
Date2026-10-28

Collecting the law into one rulebook

Can we get every rule that decides whether a Barcelona shop may become a home into one searchable file, with the exact article each rule comes from?

What we did

  • Downloaded national laws from the BOE open-data API, the 5 main parts of the Spanish building code (CTE) as PDFs, the Catalan laws from the Portal Jurídic, and the Barcelona and metropolitan plan rules (PGM, OME).
  • Cut every text into pieces (an article, a table, a paragraph) and kept the law, the article number and the web address with each piece.
  • Scanned PDFs were read with Tesseract OCR (Catalan + Spanish), column by column.

Result

2,470 pieces from 22 norms at 4 levels (Spain, Catalonia, metropolitan area, Barcelona).

What we learned

  • The CTE PDFs use a special font for ≥ and ≤; without a fix every limit lost its sign.
  • An early clean-up deleted short words like "no", which flipped the meaning of a rule. Clean-up now never deletes letters or digits.
  • Some sites (gencat, amb.cat) block scripts; those PDFs were downloaded by hand in the browser.

Decision

One file, data/rulebook.jsonl, is the only source of legal text for every later stage.

E02
Stage3 Rule cards / 9 Evaluation
Date2026-10-28

Testing 9 rules on real flats

If we write the main housing rules as code, how many real, already-built flats pass them? This tells us whether our rules are read correctly.

What we did

  • Wrote 9 rules from Decret 141/2012 (Catalan housing quality) and the Barcelona plan, each with its article.
  • Ran them on 46,937 real Swiss flats (Swiss Dwellings v3.0.0) and 11,542 plans from ResPlan.
  • Ran the shell rules on 341 Swiss commercial units (shops).

Result

32% of Swiss flats and 38% of ResPlan plans pass every rule. 97 of 341 shops pass the rules an empty shell can meet.

What we learned

  • Swiss flats are not built for Catalan rules, so most failures are expected. The useful part is which rule fails.
  • The façade rule (MPGM 2004 DA1 2.b) fails most often. Bigger flats pass more often.
  • Swiss window heights are measured from the building's base, not the room floor; this had to be corrected.

Decision

The rules work on real data; the generator must pass all 9 before a plan is shown as "works".

Cleaning the shop dataset

Which of the real shops are worth trying to convert at all?

E04
Stage6 Better plans (data)
Date2026-10-28

Learning room sizes from flats that pass

How big should each room be? We do not want to invent sizes.

What we did

  • Took only the Swiss flats that pass all 9 rules and measured their rooms, by flat type.

Result

Typical (median) and compact (25th percentile) sizes for studio, 1-, 2- and 3-bedroom flats.

What we learned

  • A typical 1-bed that passes is about 58 m² useful; a studio about 49 m².

Decision

The generator uses these learned sizes; "compact rooms" in Grasshopper switches to the smaller set.

E05
Stage6 Better plans v1
Date2026-10-28

First plan generator: flats along the façade

Given a shop's outline and façade, can the computer place the flats asked for (for example 2 × 1-bed + 1 × 2-bed) so that every flat passes every rule?

What we did

  • Living rooms and bedrooms go in a front band along the façade (daylight); kitchen, bath and hall in a band behind.
  • Every placed flat is checked against the 9 rules, each failure named with its article.
  • Asked 6 unit mixes of all 180 shops, with typical and compact room sizes.

Result

With "as many flats as fit", 144 of 180 shops get a working proposal, 360 flats in total.

What we learned

  • Bigger mixes run out of façade: 2 × 1-bed + 1 × 2-bed works in about 1 shop in 5.
  • The deep inside of a shop stays empty (see the 3D view): no light reaches it without a patio.
  • Only façades that already have windows are used.

Decision

Stage 6 v2 must add patios (PGM art. 225.7), back flats and a shared corridor.

E06
Stage4 Read a plan v1
Date2026-10-28

Reading a plan drawing

Can NOMAD take a plan image or PDF and find the shop's outline, area and street façade by itself?

What we did

  • Used the floorplan-to-3d AI model (MIT, from a team link) to label walls, doors and windows; dark lines also count as walls.
  • Straighten the plan, fill in from the paper's edge, close door and shopfront gaps, keep the inside as one outline.
  • Drew all 180 shops as test plans with known answers, plus messy copies with text, dimensions, furniture and scan noise.

Result

With one known size given: outline overlap 0.93 (1 = perfect), area within 10% on 77% of plans. Without a size the reader's own scale guess is 54% off.

What we learned

  • The model labels windows as doors, so both now count as façade openings.
  • Wide glass shopfronts let the fill leak inside; fixed by closing straight gaps along a wall line.
  • Guessing the scale is unreliable, so NOMAD always asks for one dimension (known_facade_m).

Decision

Reader v1 is in NOMAD.gh: Plan file + known_facade_m + read plan.

E07
Stage4 Read a plan / all stages
Date2026-10-28

GitHub scan and a plan-reader race

Has someone already built (and shared) the parts we need: reading a plan, flags, better plans, 3D? If so, use their code.

What we did

  • Searched GitHub for 31 repos across stages 4–7 and checked each one's licence, last update and fit (docs/github_repos.csv).
  • Downloaded MitUNet (2026, a sharper wall model) and raced 4 wall readers on 60 clean and 60 messy test plans, one size given.

Result

On messy plans: ours 83% area within 10%, MitUNet 63%, dark lines alone 87%, AI model alone 72%.

What we learned

  • MitUNet did not help on our drawings; plain dark lines are as good, because our test plans draw walls solid black.
  • Most strong plan models (Raster2Seq, MitUNet, CubiCasa) have non-commercial weights (CC BY-NC): fine for the master, not for a product.
  • No repo checks Catalan or Barcelona rules; the closest re-usable parts are IfcOpenShell/IfcTester (rules on BIM), shapely (exact circle test), OR-Tools CP-SAT and the Autodesk floorplate generator (unit mix + corridor), and IfcOpenShell + trimesh for 3D.

Decision

Keep our reader. Next tests need real scanned plans (CubiCasa5K) where AI should matter. Stage 5–7 start from the repos marked Use / Copy the method.

E08
Stage5 Check + flags
Date2026-10-28

Flags on the plan, drawn in Rhino

Can NOMAD show on the plan itself which parts are usable for housing, which rules pass or fail, and give one clear answer: YES, PARTLY or NO?

What we did

  • Wrote the Stage 5 check (src/check/flags.py): one rule card per rule with OK/FAIL, the value, and the article it comes from; a new check that every flat stays inside the shop; and the verdict.
  • Drew it as a sheet in Rhino from NOMAD.gh: the plan tinted by daylight from the façade, flats and rooms labelled, rule cards (leaders only where a rule fails), 'How it was checked' and 'Result'.
  • Added 'make sheet' and 'run inbox' to NOMAD.gh: drop plans in nomad/inbox and each one gets a sheet (PNG + numbers).
  • Ran the check on all 180 shops for three unit mixes.

Result

Asking 2 × 1-bed + 1 × 2-bed: 40 shops YES, 105 PARTLY, 43 NO. The card that fails most is 'Flats that fit' (146 shops): the shop runs out of façade or depth. 26 shops have more than 20% of their floor dark.

What we learned

  • Almost no flat that fits breaks a housing rule; the problem is space along the façade. That is Stage 6's job (patios, back flats, corridor).
  • The new 'inside the shop' check caught a generator flat that crossed the outline on a read plan.
  • Taken from GitHub for this step: nothing. The rule checks already existed in our code; IfcOpenShell/IfcTester checks BIM models and is kept for Stage 7.
  • Rhino stopped obeying /runscript at start-up after a plug-in update; a helper now types the command into a fresh Rhino (src/rhino/run_in_rhino.ps1).

Decision

Stage 5 v1 lives in NOMAD.gh. The daylight part stays as it is (Bars owns the daylight analysis). Next: Stage 6, better plans.

E09
Stage6 Better plans
Date2026-10-28

Before and after: the smallest change that houses more flats

When the flats asked for do not fit, which is the smallest building change that makes more of them fit and pass every rule?

What we did

  • Wrote Stage 6 (src/generate/better.py): try the shop as it is; then cut a light patio into the dark interior, add a 1,20 m shared corridor from the street, and line the patio with back flats. One patio at a time, kept only if it houses more passing flats; ties go to the fewest and smallest patios.
  • Rules from our rulebook: patio = Decret 141/2012 Annex 1 2.5.1 (fits a 3 m circle, at least 9 m²); corridor = CTE DB-SUA Anejo A (accessible route at least 1,20 m). A cross patio runs across narrow deep shops, with an open gallery behind it so every back flat has a door.
  • Redrew the sheets the way you asked: white, no grid, no boxes, clear Arial text, before and after side by side, the verdict large and translucent over each plan.
  • Every sheet is saved as an experiment that NOMAD.gh draws into rhino/NOMAD.3dm (layer 06 NOMAD experiments), so they all stay in the one Rhino file. Tested on a copy of the file: 7 experiments, 726 objects.

Result

Asking 2 × 1-bed + 1 × 2-bed of all 180 shops: shops where everything works 40 → 43; flats that pass 251 → 266. Asking 5 × 1-bed + 2 × 2-bed: flats that pass 306 → 347.

What we learned

  • The gain is real but small. What stops most shops is the length of street façade, not daylight depth: only 21 of 180 shops are deep enough (about 18 m) for a patio plus back flats.
  • Narrow deep shops (about 7 m wide, like the warehouse in Fig. E09.2) cannot hold a corridor and a back flat side by side: the answer stays NO, and the sheet says why.
  • Placing small flats first sometimes fits more than big flats first; the generator now tries both (no building work).
  • Taken from GitHub: nothing. The Autodesk floorplate generator's corridor idea matched ours; writing it in our own generator was less work than porting TypeScript.

Decision

Stage 6 v2 is in NOMAD.gh (the 3D shows the after plan; patios light blue, corridor grey). Next: new windows in blind street walls (needs cadastre data on which walls face the street) and Stage 7, the 3D model.

E10
Stage4 → 5 → 6 → 7 (demo)
Date2026-10-28

The demo: from a commercial plan to homes, in Rhino

Using only the rules we collected, can NOMAD take a commercial floor plan, say YES / PARTLY / NO, draw three 2D layouts with the number of homes, and build a 3D model of each?

What we did

  • Stage 4: a plan drawing is read (outline + façade) - or a shop comes from the dataset.
  • Stage 5: flags on the shop as it is, for the flats asked for (2 × 1-bed + 1 × 2-bed): every rule with its article, and the verdict.
  • Stage 6: three 2D iterations - 1 as asked, 2 as asked with compact (still legal) rooms, 3 as many homes as fit - each may add a light patio and a 1,20 m shared corridor when that houses more flats; each is checked again.
  • Stage 7: a 3D model of each iteration, built in Rhino under its plan (walls cut at 1,20 m so the rooms show).
  • Five shops (one read from a drawing) drawn by NOMAD.gh in Rhino; every one is kept in rhino/NOMAD.3dm on layer 06 NOMAD experiments. Then all 180 shops for the totals.

Result

All 180 shops, asked 2 × 1-bed + 1 × 2-bed. Stage 5 as it is: 41 YES, 104 PARTLY, 43 NO. Homes: iteration 1 266, iteration 2 304, iteration 3 466 (YES in 154 shops; at least one home in 157 shops).

What we learned

  • Compact rooms (still legal) add about one home in seven; asking for 'as many as fit' instead of a fixed mix adds the most.
  • About 30 shops cannot hold even one home: too small, too narrow, or too little façade.
  • The audit found and fixed: stale plan readings, a memory leak in Rhino, pictures not embedded in the 3dm, experiments that could drift from their pictures, a silent scale guess and the wall band covering complex plans (docs/audit_2026-10-28.md).

Decision

This is what we show. Next: new windows in blind walls (cadastre data), Bars's daylight analysis, and IFC / glTF export of the Stage 7 models.

E11
Stage7.2 (after 7.1 zoning)
Date2026-10-28

From zoning to walls: doors and windows in Rhino

Can the Stage 7.1 zoning (each room a coloured block) be turned into a real model with walls, doors and windows, so the rooms and halls read as spaces?

What we did

  • Same layouts as Stage 6 and the same E05 colours: each room floor in its colour (red if it fails a rule), patios light blue, the shared corridor grey.
  • Walls 2,70 m high with no roof: the shell 30 cm, partitions 10 cm, placed in the 20 cm gap NOMAD leaves between rooms so two neighbours make one wall.
  • Windows in the street facade, 60% of each room's width and 1,5 m tall (sill 0,90 m), as light-blue glass: the same window the Stage 5 check assumes (Decret 141/2012 Annex 1 3.9.1, 1/8 of the floor).
  • A door of 0,80 m from every room to its flat's hall (or living room), and an entry door of 0,90 m in the street facade for each flat (flats are entered from the street).
  • Drawn by NOMAD.gh under each zoning model (layer 06 NOMAD experiments::<shop>::walls iteration k) and captured from above in Rhino.

Result

All five demo shops built: for the large shop, iteration 3 has 12 homes, 32 windows, 12 entries; every layout shows its rooms, halls and openings.

What we learned

  • Blocks are best for reading the zoning (which room is which); walls are best for reading the space (how you walk in, where the light comes from). We keep both, one after the other.
  • A steep view is needed: from low down the walls hide the coloured floors.
  • Door positions are a design assumption (centre of the shared wall), not a rule check.

Decision

Stage 7 is now two steps: 7.1 zoning, 7.2 walls, doors, windows. Next: check door widths and the hall turning circle against the rules, then IFC export.

E12
Stage3 (zoning)
Date2026-10-29

Zoning: can each empty shop in Barcelona become housing at all?

Before any plan is drawn: does the urban zoning of each empty ground-floor premises in Barcelona admit housing?

What we did

  • Took the 6,149 empty premises (for rent, for sale or both) of the Ajuntament's 2024 ground-floor census (Open Data BCN, CC BY 4.0).
  • Downloaded the 12,583 zoning polygons of Barcelona from the Mapa Urbanístic de Catalunya (MUC, Departament de Territori), once, 2,000 per request.
  • Matched every premises to the zone it stands in (point in polygon, EPSG:25831). The census point is the shop door: 60 doors fell in the street polygon and take the zone of the building behind (nearest non-street zone within 8 m).
  • Answered each zone with PGM art. 225.7 (MPGM 1999): housing on the ground floor is admitted in the zones built along street lines (12, 13a, 13b, 14b, 15); industrial zones and systems (streets, parks, facilities) are not; specific volumetry, isolated buildings and 22@ depend on their own plan.

Result

5,026 of 6,149 empty premises (82 %) stand in a zone that admits housing on the ground floor (13a 2,334, 12 1,383, 13b 1,259, 15 49); 817 depend on their zone's own plan; 306 do not (facilities, parks, industry, port). In the Eixample: 1,160 of 1,224 (95 %).

What we learned

  • Zoning is rarely the problem in the dense city: four in five empty shops pass the first gate. The rules inside the shop (façade, light, size) decide.
  • The 'check' group is not random: specific volumetry (635) and 22@ (58) need the detailed plan, which the MUC does not carry.
  • Art. 225.7 adds a condition NOMAD does not check yet: a room facing the inner block needs that part unbuilt, at least 6 m deep and 4,5 m wide.

Decision

Zoning is NOMAD's first gate (step 3, now running). Next: the inner-block condition of art. 225.7, and the 817 'check' premises against their detailed plans.

E13
Stage3 + 9 (rule cards, evaluation)
Date2026-10-29

Rule cards and a golden set: does NOMAD use the right numbers?

Does every number NOMAD checks match the official text, with the right article, and does it check every rule it should?

What we did

  • Wrote 16 rule cards (rules/rules.json), each quoting its sentence of the official text from the rulebook. Two automatic checks: the quote is word for word in the official text, and the number is in the quote. 15 pass; the CTE corridor card goes to review (the PDF table was garbled).
  • Wrote a golden set of 16 questions (eval/golden_set.csv): 10 from the team's gold cards (Exp 2) and 6 from the text, every answer found word for word in its source.
  • Scored NOMAD's code against it: the value each check uses and the article it cites. Also scored plain keyword search (BM25) of the rulebook: does the right article come first?
  • Fixed what the evaluation found, and scored again.

Result

Before the fixes: 12 of 16 right (3 rules not checked; 2,50 m used where a shop conversion needs 2,40 m; one article wrong). After: 16 of 16, every citation right. Keyword search finds the right article first for 13 of 16 questions and in the top 5 for 15.

What we learned

  • The golden set found a real bug: 8 shops of 2,44-2,45 m had been thrown out. As a change of use (Decret 141/2012 Annex 4 group C) they only need 2,40 m: the dataset grows from 180 to 188 shops.
  • The living-dining-kitchen minimum of 20 m² (Annex 1 3.7.1, in the team's gold cards) is marked 'No vigent' in the consolidated text: it no longer applies.
  • Annex 4 group C also exempts Annex 1 section 2 for a shop conversion, so the patio size rule is stricter than needed (safe).

Decision

Three checks added (living room on the façade >= 2,20 m, wet rooms >= 2,20 m high, hall >= 1 m), 2,40 m for conversions, art. 323 cited for zone 13. Re-score the golden set after every rule change.

E15
Stage4 (Exp 1, real scans)
Date2026-10-29

The plan reader on 398 real scanned plans

Our plan reader scored 0.93 on plans we drew ourselves. How does it do on real scanned floor plans it has never seen?

What we did

  • Took the CubiCasa5K test split: 400 real Finnish plan scans (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0) that our wall model was never trained or tuned on (checked: 0 overlap with its train and validation lists).
  • Built each answer from the plan's SVG: the indoor rooms and the walls between them, without balconies, terraces or garages, shrunk by one wall thickness (the first version split thick log walls into pieces, and was redone).
  • Raced four wall readers with one known size: ours (AI model + dark lines), dark lines only, the AI model only, and MitUNet from GitHub.

Result

On 398 real scans: dark lines only 0.71 median overlap (26 % of areas within 10 %); ours 0.69 (20 %); MitUNet 0.68 (19 %); the AI model alone 0.61 (19 %). On our own drawings the same reader scored 0.93 (77 %).

What we learned

  • Real scans are much harder: coloured walls, open plans, text and dimension lines, and several floors on one sheet.
  • On real scans the AI wall models do not help yet: plain dark lines do best. Both AI models were trained on clean drawings of the same plans, not on scans.
  • A wrong answer key can look like a bad reader: the first answers were too small, and fixing them changed the scores.

Decision

Keep asking for one size and showing what was found so the user can correct it. Next: fine-tune the wall model on scans (CubiCasa's train split), and test on CVC-FP (122 scans from the UAB, Barcelona).

NOMAD on Barcelona's real empty shops

Does the whole pipeline work on real Barcelona buildings, not only test plans?

What we did

  • Matched the 6,149 empty ground-floor premises of the 2024 census to Catastro INSPIRE buildings, built each shop's shell (street façade = edge facing a street polygon in the MUC), cleaned the errors, and ran NOMAD's check and three layouts on every shop that is alone on its ground floor.

E18
Stage4
Date2026-10-29

Fine-tuning the plan reader on real scans

Does training the floorplan-to-3d model on real CubiCasa5K scans make it read real scanned plans better?

What we did

  • Fetched 182 training and 40 check scans from the CubiCasa5K train/val splits (never the test split), read straight from the Zenodo zip.
  • Trained the published weights for 8 rounds on the laptop GPU (RTX 3060, about 2 minutes).
  • Graded it on the same 398 real test scans as before, with one known size.

Result

On the 40 check scans the wall overlap rose from 0.098 to 0.118. On the 398 test scans it got worse: outline overlap 0.617 (published model 0.685, dark lines 0.709), area within 10 % on 11 % (published 20 %, dark lines 26 %).

What we learned

  • 182 scans and 8 rounds are not enough: the model learned the new scans' walls a little and forgot part of what it did well.
  • Dark pixels are still the best wall reader on scans.

Decision

Keep the published weights. A real fine-tune needs the full train split (about 4,000 scans) and a longer run; the download is the bottleneck (about 1-2 scans a minute).

E20
Stage5
Date2026-10-29

Bars's daylight model on Barcelona's real empty shops

How much daylight do the real empty shops get, and how deep is the liveable part?

What we did

  • Took Bars's Experiment 1 unchanged (iaac-maai/nomad: 50 Ladybug/Honeybee daylight simulations, XGBoost, his features and assumptions).
  • Measured each of NOMAD's real shop shells: depth from the street façade, façade width, distance and height of the building across the street (a ray into the Catastro buildings).
  • Trained his second target too (share of floor with daylight factor >= 2 %) to get the lit depth: how far from the window the floor stays at DF >= 2 %.

Result

1,650 shops: median predicted daylight factor 1.2 %; 420 well lit (>= 2 %), 858 some daylight, 372 dark (< 1 %). Median lit depth 2.95 m (our fixed assumption was 5.5 m). On the 111 shops Bars also modelled, geometry agrees within centimetres and the predicted DF median 1.15 % vs his 1.12 %. Only 211 shops are fully inside his training ranges.

What we learned

  • Most ground floors are darker than the 5.5 m light band assumed: daylight, not the rules, limits liveable depth.
  • Daylight is not a legal threshold in Decret 141/2012 (that is window area >= 1/8), so it is used as a liveability signal, not a fail.

Decision

Can NOMAD fill each flat with a real, architect-made plan instead of inventing the rooms, and still pass the law?

  • Plan-graph checks: every room reached from the entrance hall, no bedroom only through another, a bathroom reached without crossing a bedroom, living room and bedrooms on a façade.

920 real flats NOMAD can use (51 studios, 290 one-beds, 454 two-beds, 125 three-beds); fits score 77-98. On 40 real Barcelona shops: 54 legal homes in 28 shops (old generator: 70 homes in 28 shops, with narrower invented rooms).

  • Real one-sided flats are studios and 1-beds; real 2- and 3-beds have windows on two façades. A shop lit only from the street fits mostly small flats.
  • Real flats are entered from a hall behind them, not from the street: the shop needs a lobby and a shared hall, as a real building does.
  • What is left in a deep shop has no façade at all: it can only serve the building (storage, bike room).

Decision

Furniture, your own plans, generated plans and an assistant (the rest of the Finch list)

Can every flat be furnished as the law asks, can users add their own plans, what happens when no real plan fits, and can a chat assistant make bulk edits?

What we did

  • Furniture (src/generate/furnish.py): every flat gets what Decret 141/2012 Annex 1 asks for: a wardrobe 0,60 m deep, 1 m long over 6 m², 1,5 m over 8 m² (3.10.1); the bedroom square still free of door swings and the wardrobe (3.8); a worktop with sink and cooker and 1,00 m free in front (3.15.c, 3.7.3); WC, basin and shower (3.15.b, 3.11.1); a washing machine place (3.15.d); and the 1,20 m circle free of door swings and fixed equipment (3.4.1.e). Decision

Keep: every flat furnished and checked; own plans through DXF or JSON; generated plans only when no real plan fits, always marked; the assistant only acts through the page's own tools.

  • The law: a whole building is Decret 141/2012 Annex 4 Group B (total change of use). Annex 1 applies with patio surfaces -10 % and a 2,40 m clear height.   • - With more than one level, each street door becomes a core: two 1,00 m flights, a lift for a 1,00 x 1,25 m cabin, a 1,50 m landing, and room for the community room.

Of those not in use by the census hint, 893 stand in a zone that admits housing (259 warehouses, 482 commercial, 152 office) and 403 in a zone whose own plan decides. On 40 empty warehouses in zones that admit housing (6 m clear height, mezzanine): 48 homes in 13 buildings, all passing Group B; patios used in 5. Most of the rest are small sheds and garages (50-300 m²). An empty 365 m² nau on Av. Marquès de Castellbell gets 8 homes on 2 levels.

  • The mezzanine doubles the homes in a 6 m tall nau. The stair-and-lift core costs one flat's width per street door.

The 29 October crits: real daylight, liveable zones, temporary homes, a city view, the metropolitan area, precedents

What did the three critics ask for that NOMAD still did not do, and can it do it now?

What we did

  • Real daylight in the check (critic B, 'people don't need to live in a jail'): the generator and the final check now use Bars's predicted lit depth for each shop (DF >= 2 %, E20), or 5,5 m labelled as an assumption where there is none. A living room or bedroom passes only with at least half its floor within that depth (NOMAD liveability, not law).
  • Liveable zones ('blue spots'): the website draws the floor in lit / service / dark bands in the first tile of every design ('As it is: where light reaches').
  • Temporary / emergency homes (critics A and C): read the law first. 'Allotjament temporal de protecció' is only allowed on public land for facilities (TRLU art. 34.5 quater), not in a private shop. The route that fits a shop is shared-space housing (Llei 18/2007 art. 3.p and 18 bis, DL 50/2020, DT 10a, Circular 8 AHC): a private space of at least 24 m² plus shared rooms of at least 6 m² per home (6 m², 2,45 m circle, lit). In Barcelona the private space plus its share of the shared rooms must reach 40 m² (MPGM 2004 DA1 2.a with art. 18 bis.2, the stricter reading). Density may be exceeded for homes for groups needing social attention (MPGM 2004 DA2). src/generate/shared.py: small studios, the best-lit ones become shared living rooms and kitchens until every home has its proportional share. Website: 'Kind of homes' in step 2.
  • For the city (critic A: policy makers): src/check/city_runs.py ran NOMAD on all 1,694 clean empty shops and all 1,206 warehouses, commercial and office buildings not in use by the census hint. Website section 'For the city': the homes the city needs, then the fewest places that reach it, by district, and what to avoid (not allowed, not liveable, keep it a shop, no legal home fits).
  • Beyond Barcelona (critic C): src/zoning/amb.py read the Catastro buildings of all 36 AMB towns (links from the Catastro's index) and their MUC zoning. Towns with their own plan are answered by the harmonised MUC code: A1 industrial, A2 tertiary and N non-urbanisable = no; R residential and other codes = the local plan decides.
  • Precedents (critic A): a website section with the sourced numbers (Barcelona, Madrid, Paris, England, APROP), each marked official or secondary.

Result

Real daylight: on 40 real shops, 48 legal homes in 22 shops instead of 57 in 28; 9 homes in 6 shops had rooms mostly in the dark. City: 838 of 1,694 empty shops give 1,405 homes; unused buildings: 249 warehouses give 1,672 homes, 261 commercial buildings 3,414, 211 office buildings 8,077 (several storeys). In zones that admit housing: 6,442 homes; 1,000 homes need 13 places. Avoid: 1,159 not allowed, 949 not liveable, 464 keep it a shop, 739 where no legal home fits. AMB: 201,339 buildings; 15,885 warehouses, commercial and office buildings with no homes; 3,769 in a zone that admits housing, 2,177 where the local plan decides. Shared-space homes: in Barcelona's 40 m² rule they give as many homes as full flats (large demo shop: 6), smaller and with shared rooms.

What we learned

  • Real daylight removes about one home in six: the 5,5 m assumption was too kind.
  • Most homes in the city are in unused office buildings with several storeys, not in shops.
  • Shared-space housing is the legal small-home route for a shop, but Barcelona's 40 m² per home keeps it from adding homes; with the 36 m² of Decret 141/2012 it would.

Decision

Keep: real daylight for every place with a prediction, the liveable zones on the plans, shared-space homes as a choice, the city view and the AMB layer. To confirm with the city: the 40 m² reading for shared-space homes and the density exception.

NOMAD's own plan AI: trained on 15,545 real flats, every plan checked

Can an AI trained on real plans draw flats that fit a shop better than stretching the few real plans that pass?

AI library: 227 plans from 5,760 slots. 40 real shops with Bars's daylight: 48 legal homes in 22 shops before; 52 in 25 with the ranking fix; 57 in 28 with the AI plans (36 AI flats used); median floor left unused per shop 134 -> 127 -> 101 m².

  • Straight walls matter more than quantity: most raw AI pictures need cleaning, and only clean ones are kept.

Keep: AI plans in the library, marked 'NOMAD AI', only when they pass every check; the ranking fix for all plans. Next: request RPLAN / LIFULL, train longer, and a diffusion model for more variety.
